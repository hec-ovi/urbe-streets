# Streets 0.14.0

Builds a shared street catalogue and city placements from saved Atlas 0.26.0 with planning reservations 2.1.0.

`build(request,options):Promise<NativeStreetBuild>` from [src/index.ts](src/index.ts), also exported as `buildNative`. Inputs: [request](src/schema/native-request.ts), [material binding](src/schema/native-materials.ts). Output: [manifest 0.5.0](src/schema/native-result.ts).

Required: saved blueprint object or JSON path, integer seed, design `{version:'native-1.0.0',wear:0..1}` and `options.nativeMaterials`. Paths come from the caller. Blueprint files hash their exact bytes; object inputs, material bindings and delegated infrastructure hash ordered UTF8 JSON.stringify. Piece hashes cover exact GLB bytes. Archive indexes fail explicitly.

## Shared catalogue

Every build publishes identical `streets/kit.json` and `streets/pieces/**/*.glb`, including unused profiles. The [catalogue](src/construction/units/profiles.json) defines 4 m single lane streets, 7 m two lane streets, 14 m avenues, 17.4 m avenues with 3.4 m medians, and pedestrian alleys across ordinary, luxury and industrial zones. Motor streets have 4.2 m paving, 0.2 m curbs and 0.5 m gutters. Alley half widths are 1.8, 2.5 and 2 m respectively.

The complete inventory has 255 pieces: 105 segments, 66 junction arm pieces, 42 centres, 30 props and 12 overlays. Each profile has plain 8 m segments plus plain 4 m and 2 m closures. Every motor profile, median profiles included, also supplies an 8 m road core and a plain 2 m core closure. Four kerb finishes supply 8 m walks, 2 m walk closures, 6 m parking slots and two 4 m end returns. Kerb pieces support both motor classes. The two ordinary road profiles supply the court under a highway deck in 8 m and 2 m lengths: one road-level slab of the `hex-orange` surface (falling back to `district-hex`), sampled in world metres, with no lanes, paint, gutters or curbs. Each arm starts at the crossing carriageway edge; the cross street width changes its placement position. Every motor profile supplies an arm core, its carriageway alone; each kerb finish supplies the two sides of an arm, `junction/side/<finish>/left` (the arm's positive local Z) and `right`, gutter, curb and walk with their half of the corner up to the diagonal seam, authored beside a 7 m street and offset to the arm's own half width; and, per finish and motor street width, the far kerb where a street ends at a T: gutter, curb and walk straight across, between the neighbouring arms' corner seams (`junction/far-kerb/<class>/<finish>`). The whole arm and return per profile remain in the catalogue. An alley's return stays a 4.9 m walk stub. Median profiles leave their 3.4 m band open. Each zone supplies median island units: a 2 m nose and plain 2 m and 8 m islands. Centres cover unordered motor and mixed profile pairs; pedestrian-only centres use source infill. Rotation handles reversed pairs. Zone materials and diagonal corner seams are baked. Three plain right-triangle prisms, asphalt, district-hex and concrete, fit saved fields. Stripe overlays place Atlas crossing polygons and approach stop lines at their authored stations, as tiles of a 0.5 m or 0.3 m wide stripe one paint turn (2.7 m) long, as many as the stripe's length holds tiles near that long, each scaled to an equal share. Road dashes have one phase: local X from 0 to 4 m painted, then 4 m clear. Closures have no paint.

Paint and gutter UVs keep their texture's proportions. Paint publishes mask UVs at one scale along and across a mark, 2.7 m per turn: strips `U = along / 2.7 + phase, V = 0.155 + across / 2.7`, bars with along and across exchanged, arrows `V = 0.5 + across / 2.7`, parking slot lines and stripe tiles alike. A gutter maps its whole width across one curb band scan and, along it, eight times that width per scan (the scan's 2 m by 0.25 m proportions), each scan starting where a whole turn of it begins along the face; curb tops and faces keep 2 m along and 0.2 m across or up.

Overlays comprise seven combinations of through, left and right arrows, drain overlays for 0.5 m and 0.7 m gutter and curb bands, one scan quad and two white stripe tiles. The 0.5 m drain overlay draws the visible grate, dark curb mouth and 2 m cover hatch over source inlets; the 0.7 m overlay is the 2 m cover hatch alone, because the district inlet is the whole drain station with its own flush grate and curb throats. The hatch is a `drainCover` with metre UVs over its 2 x 2 m. An access cassette's perforated plates between its solid bars are `drainGrate`; the bars and frames keep their own surfaces. Overlays sit on the receiving surfaces and have no collision. Props retain the original hardware geometry, except the marquee run pieces and the district inlet top, which are authored ([hardware](src/construction/hardware/CONTRACT.md)). Catalogue geometry is independent of city, seed, wear and material binding; only fallback surface names follow the binding.

The budget is at most 256 pieces and 3,000,000 bytes for all GLBs plus compact kit JSON. Exceeding either budget fails with measured totals before publishing a bundle. `report.profiles` lists widths mapped to the nearest profile within their class and zone, with roadId, profileId, requestedWidth, width and signed delta in metres. Catalogue order breaks ties. An instance whose complete surface roles or heights disagree with saved ground is fitted with source infill instead. Width mappings remain diagnostic; saved ground is fully covered.

## Placements

Kit [1.2.0](schemas/street-kit.schema.json), placements [1.2.0](schemas/street-placement.schema.json). Shared [types](src/schema/street-kit.ts). Piece files resolve relative to `streets/kit.json`. Coordinates are X right, Y up, Z forward. Each piece supplies bounds, footprint, surfaces, collision flag, triangle count, bytes and SHA256.

Each placement carries only `piece`, `position`, `rotationY`, optional `scale`, `cell`, `ownerId`, `ownerIds` and optional shader values `tint`, `wear`, `scan`, `text`. Draw every primitive in the referenced GLB. Apply complete GLB node transforms, positive local XYZ scale (default `[1,1,1]`), positive Y rotation in radians, then world position. Rendering and collision use the complete transformed geometry. `placementFootprint(piece,placement)` applies that same placement transform to the piece footprint.

| Shader value | Meaning and default |
| --- | --- |
| `tint` | Linear RGB multiplier in 0 to 1, default `[1,1,1]`. |
| `wear` | Sampled wear amount in 0 to 1, default 0. |
| `scan` | `{offset:[u,v],scale:[u,v]}` applied as `uv * scale + offset`, default identity. |
| `text` | Glyph indices into `kit.glyphs`, default empty. |

The scan piece is one unit quad with local UV `[x+0.5,0.5-z]`. Placement scale supplies its dimensions. `kit.scanAtlas` orders four equal horizontal UV cells: damage, fracture, repair and oil. Resolve each cell to its native surface maps once when preparing the shared scan material; UV cell selection happens in the shader. A marquee run is `prop/marquee-run/cap-start`, two or three `prop/marquee-run/segment-2m` or `segment-1m` pieces and `prop/marquee-run/cap-end`, end to end at one rotation in increasing local X; the two caps mirror each other and cut their outer curb-side corners. Each segment carries an LED field on its own `marquee-led` slot, the binding's `led-matrix` surface, with UVs in metres: u runs 0 to 1.92 (0.92 on the 1 m closure) from left to right seen from the road, v runs 0 to 0.23 from road to curb. Every segment of a run carries the run's message in `text`; caps carry none. The binding's LED effect letters the glyphs along u inside its glyph band and scrolls them; glyph i samples column `i%8` and row `floor(i/8)` of the 8 by 6 letter atlas. Only lit dots emit; the face and unlit dots take scene light. Lighting is never baked: GLBs carry no textures or vertex light. No placement value changes geometry.

Grade streets continue through collinear underpass nodes without a junction cut. A highway's grade corridor below its delegated deck is a court, never a carriageway: the under-highway slab of the road profile of its width, in 8 m and 2 m lengths, between the kerbs of the blocks either side; at a grade junction box its arm is that court with the blocks' own arm sides, and where a grade street passes beneath it stops at the saved underpass kerb line. Junction arms end 18 m beyond the crossing carriageway edge, exactly where runs start. Every block keeps one sidewalk finish all round: each motor run is a road core with kerb walks either side in the finish of the block that side borders (8 m walks, 2 m walk closures, a scaled 2 m closure for a fractional remainder), each arm is its core with the finished side of each bordering block, and each far kerb takes the finish of the block across; the block is the owner whose saved sidewalk holds the walking band. Straight runs use 8 m cores, 2 m core closures end to end for the rest, then a 2 m core closure scaled along X for a fractional remainder. A district parking bay holds one to six 6 m slots in a rectangular notch; its footprint is that notch or the notch with 45 degree returns over its 2 m end runs. It places one 6 m kerb strip per slot and two end returns at its saved frontage stations and inward side. Each return covers 2 m of bay and its adjacent 2 m support. The bay, gutter, curb and walking fields must match the saved ground exactly. Road cores preserve the original lane paint phase; independent walks fill the remaining kerbs in the block's finish. Slot boundaries carry paint at 6 m pitch, rear curbs use 2 m units, and the inner walking band stays 2.2 m wide. Kerb finishes follow ordinary, luxury blue, luxury red or industrial yellow owners. The drawn ends follow the rectangular notch ground as authored. `closures` records roadId, length, remaining stations, piece counts and fittedLength. Station aprons tile pedestrian pieces around shafts; edge tiles scale to their rectangular receiving area. Each saved median island is a nose at either end with 8 m and 2 m islands between, at its Atlas stations. Where a highway corridor ends at a street passing beneath, its 0.7 m gutter and curb across the corridor is concrete infill; elevated structures stay delegated. Infill partitions each uncovered source field into right triangles and scales shared prisms to its exact outline and levels. Leftover carriageway takes patched asphalt (`asphalt-patched`, piece `infill/asphalt`), or district-hex on luxury roads; both sample world XZ, so a scaled prism shows no stretch. Other leftovers take concrete.

`cell` is `[floor(x/128),floor(z/128)]`. Cells do not split pieces. Every placement identifies its primary owner and all ground owners touched by its footprint. Feature records retain source identities, anchors and the index of their prop placement in `features[].placement`. Shaft and station references, original ground indices and delegated infrastructure remain in the manifest.

Coverage and collision checks start from whole transformed piece footprints. Surface instances must fit their complete receiving roles and heights, have disjoint interiors and clear station shafts. A raised collision surface above 0.05 m cannot intersect a level-zero driving lane. Source infill covers the exact remainder without repainting highway ground as an avenue. Props follow the kerbs these surfaces preserve; complete LED runs respect fractional closure exclusions. `report.degraded` lists every parking bay the box cannot build, with its authored id and the reason; the bay leaves the placements and the ordinary segment keeps its ground covered. Repeated physical surface coverage is measured at 0.000001 m2 precision; any positive area fails with `E_INVARIANT`. `ground.cover.outsideArea` measures the union outside retained reservations. Paint and other overlays contribute no ground coverage.

## Materials and output

GLBs contain no textures. They use shared indexed streams and require `KHR_mesh_quantization` and `EXT_meshopt_compression`. A constructed surface the binding lacks resolves through a fixed fallback: `marquee-channel` and `marquee-cap` to `darkMetal`, `marquee-frame` to `ochre`, `marquee-lip` to `concrete`, `drainCover` to `tread`, `drainGrate` to `perforated` and `asphalt-patched` to `asphalt`. Kit surfaces and GLB materials carry the resolved name; a surface with neither fails with `E_INVALID_PARAMS`. `marquee-led` has no fallback, since no other surface letters metre UVs: the binding must carry it as a `led-matrix` surface (native Materials 0.18.0), and an older binding fails the build with `E_INVALID_PARAMS` naming `marquee-led`. Register MeshoptDecoder. Decoded positions stay within 1 mm of authored Float32 positions. Materials carry `streetNativeSurface`; primitives carry `streetCollision` and `streetSource`. Attributes include position, normal, UV, `_STREET_WEAR` and `_STREET_HEIGHT`. [Assets contract](src/assets/CONTRACT.md).

Shared vertex wear is neutral. Streets samples the wear field at each placement anchor and publishes the result in `wear`. The manifest retains the field with `application: instance` for inspection and carries the complete native material snapshot.

Construction and encoding run sequentially in the calling thread. Equal requests produce byte identical manifests and placements. Without outDir, assets contains JSON and GLB bytes. With outDir, the destination must be new and its parent must exist; manifest.json is written last and assets is empty. Manifest mode emits the same metadata with JSON assets only. Statistics count unique GLB bytes and exact compact placement JSON bytes.

Errors: `E_INVALID_PARAMS` for requests, bindings and IO; `E_UNSUPPORTED_ARCHITECTURE` for a plan the box cannot read, meaning its versions, sections or malformed profiles; `E_UNSATISFIABLE` for excluded receiving land; `E_INVARIANT` for coverage, budgets or export failures. [StreetsError](src/errors.ts).

Dependencies: [Atlas](../atlas/CONTRACT.md), [native Materials 0.18.0](../materials/sources/streets/scene-native/CONTRACT.md), and the boxes in [INDEX](docs/INDEX.md). No renderer or sibling runtime imports.
